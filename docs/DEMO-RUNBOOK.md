# Demo Runbook — La Sabrosita Bakery

**A 12-minute demo that closes the sale.** Read this the night before. Run through it once, out loud, before you're in the room.

---

## Before you leave

- [ ] `npm run demo` works from a clean clone. Test it once more.
- [ ] **Turn wifi off and confirm the whole demo still runs.** Bakery wifi will betray you.
- [ ] Laptop charged, charger in the bag.
- [ ] Open the site on **your phone** too (same wifi, or `next dev -H 0.0.0.0` and hit your laptop's LAN IP). More than 70% of their traffic is mobile — showing it on a phone is more persuasive than showing it on a laptop.
- [ ] Print `docs/PITCH-MONEY-MATH.md` — one page, leave it with them.
- [ ] Have their **current site open in a second tab**, on a phone. The contrast is the pitch.
- [ ] Click "Reiniciar demo" so you start pristine.
- [ ] Know the reset button's location without looking.

---

## Who you're talking to

**Argentina Ortega** — the founder. Salvadoran. Started in her own kitchen with four customers. She thinks about bread, her family, and whether this is worth the money. **Talk to her in Spanish and show her the Spanish site.**

**Eduardo and Mario Dawson** — her sons. Left construction to invest in their mother's business. They think about growth, cost, and whether this is a real system or a toy. **The admin dashboard and the food-cost screen are for them.**

**Jenny Dawson** — daughter-in-law, quoted in their own materials saying they want to go beyond the Hispanic market and become "a multicultural store." **The bilingual site is literally her stated strategy.** Say so.

---

## Open cold — do not open the laptop yet (2 min)

Three facts, in this order. Let each land.

> **1.** "Your Google listing has 663 reviews and 4.3 stars. Nobody owns it — it still says 'Claim this business.' Nobody has ever replied to a single review. That's free to fix and I can start it today."

> **2.** *(hand them your phone with their own Products page open)* "Your website says a quesadilla entera is $6.80. DoorDash says $13.99. Every price on your site is about half. People check the site, drive over, and get a surprise at the counter."

> **3.** "You supply about 250 stores. There's no way for any of them to order except calling you."

Then, only then: *"Let me show you what I built."*

---

## The demo, in order

### 1 · The phone, side by side (90 sec)

Their current site on one phone. The new one on the other. Do not narrate. Let them scroll.

Then, one line: *"Setenta por ciento de la gente que los busca está en el teléfono, caminando, decidiendo en veinte segundos."*

Point out only three things:
- The hero says what you sell, where you are, and that it's baked this morning
- **Pedir · Menú · Llamar · Cómo llegar** — always at the bottom, one tap each
- The language toggle. Tap it. Watch Argentina's face.

### 2 · The menu (2 min)

Scroll it. Let the photos do the work.

- *"Cuarenta y cinco productos, con foto, en español y en inglés."*
- Search `concha`. Then search `pan dulce`. Then search `quesadilla`.
- *"Ahorita, si alguien busca 'pan dulce cerca de mí' en Google, ustedes no salen — porque no hay nada que Google pueda leer. El Globo tampoco. El Sol tampoco. Las Delicias tampoco. **Nadie en Richmond tiene esto.**"*

That last sentence is the most important one in the demo. Say it slowly.

### 3 · The cake configurator (2 min)

Do the whole thing. Quinceañera → 2 pisos → tres leches → fresa → fondant → *"Felices 15, Sofía"* → pick a pickup slot → deposit.

Two things to point at:

- **The lead time changes as you choose.** *"Dos pisos con fondant: siete días. El sistema no le va a prometer a nadie un pastel que ustedes no pueden hacer."*
- **The inscription, quoted back.** *"Aquí, exactamente como lo escribió la clienta. Con el acento en Sofía. Ya no se pierde nada por teléfono."*

At the pay screen: *"Esto es demo, no cobra nada. En vivo, aquí entra el depósito."*

### 4 · The Mensajes drawer (60 sec)

Open it. Show the confirmation email and the SMS, fully rendered, in Spanish.

*"Esto le llega a la clienta en el momento. Y a las 24 horas, un recordatorio. Y si contesta '2', se cancela sola."*

### 5 · The 86 moment (60 sec) — do not skip this

Portal → Panadero → 86 board → toggle **Flan** off.
Back to the public menu → refresh → **the flan is gone.**

*"Se acabó el flan a las once de la mañana. Un botón. Nadie lo pide en línea el resto del día."*

This is the moment where it stops being a website.

### 6 · The production board (60 sec)

*"Mañana necesitan 84 conchas de pedidos, más 180 de par. El sistema ya lo sumó. Y les avisa que faltan cuatro libras de mantequilla antes de que empiecen."*

### 7 · Food cost — this one is for the sons (2 min)

Admin → Costos.

*"Una concha cuesta treinta y nueve centavos hacerla. La venden a $1.75. Setenta y siete por ciento de margen."*

Then open the simulator and move butter up 15%.

*"Si sube la mantequilla quince por ciento, este número — y estos ocho productos — cambian solos. Ahorita, ¿alguien sabe cuánto cuesta hacer una concha?"*

Wait for the answer. Nobody knows. That silence is the sale.

### 8 · The dashboard and the commission card (90 sec)

Admin → dashboard. Point at the commission-saved card.

*"Los pedidos que entran por su propia página cuestan 2.9% más treinta centavos. Por DoorDash, entre veinte y treinta por ciento. Este número lo van a ver cada mes."*

Be honest here — say it out loud: *"Esta cifra es un estimado. Con su estado de cuenta de DoorDash les doy el número real."*

Then the P&L: *"Costo de insumos, mano de obra, costo primo. Esto es lo que un contador quiere ver."*

### 9 · Wholesale (90 sec) — the biggest one

*"Doscientas cincuenta tiendas."*

Show the line sheet in cases. Show the standing order. Show the route sheet — **printed, in delivery order, with a signature line.** Show the aging report.

*"Su pedido de siempre, un botón. La hoja de ruta se imprime y se sube a la camioneta. Y aquí ven quién les debe y desde cuándo."*

Then: *"Ninguna panadería en este mercado tiene esto. Ninguna."*

### 10 · Close (60 sec)

Back to the phone, on the home page.

*"Esto es lo que la clienta ve. Y detrás, ustedes ven lo que cuesta hacerlo, qué se vendió, qué se tiró, y qué quedó."*

Then hand them the one-pager and give them the three things they need to decide.

## What to say when they ask

**"¿Cuánto cuesta?"**
Have your number ready before you walk in. Anchor against what they already spend: marketplace commission, and the hours currently spent taking cake orders by phone.

**"¿Cuánto se tarda?"**
Phase 1 the site, Phase 2 ordering, Phase 3 the back office, Phase 4 wholesale. Show them the phase table in `00-INTEL-AUDIT-PLAN.md` §4.7. Do not promise dates you have not costed.

**"¿Estos son nuestros precios?"**
No — and this is a selling point. *"Todo lo que ven es de ejemplo. El sistema **no deja publicar** un precio que ustedes no me hayan confirmado. Literalmente no compila."* Show them `CONFIRM_WITH_CLIENT` if they're technical.

**"¿Y si no sabemos usar computadora?"**
The portal is in Spanish, the buttons are large, and you'll deliver a printed guide plus short videos, in Spanish, and sit with them for the first week. Say exactly that.

**"¿Podemos quedarnos con DoorDash?"**
Yes. Keep it for real delivery. Just stop paying a quarter of the ticket on people who were already driving over.

**"¿Quién es dueño de esto?"**
They are. Stripe in their name, Google profile in their name, domain in their name, and a CSV export of every table in the admin. Say it plainly — it's the thing agencies get wrong and owners have been burned by.

---

## Do not

- Do not claim a payment was processed or a message was sent. The banner says demo; keep saying demo.
- Do not present the seeded sales figures as their numbers. Say "de ejemplo" every time the P&L is on screen.
- Do not promise a search ranking or a revenue number.
- Do not open the laptop before you've said the three opening facts.
- Do not demo on wifi you don't control.
- Do not go past 15 minutes. If they're engaged, stop demoing and start talking about scope.

---

## Leave behind

1. `docs/PITCH-MONEY-MATH.md`, printed, one page.
2. The Google Business Profile finding, in writing, with the claim steps — **give them this whether or not they buy.** It costs you nothing, it's worth real money to them, and it is the single most credible thing you can do.
3. The confirmation list from `00-INTEL-AUDIT-PLAN.md` §7, trimmed to the five blocking items: hours, prices, primary phone, founding year, legal entity name.

---

## The morning after

Send one email. Their real hours question, the price gap, and the GBP claim link. No pitch. Then wait.
