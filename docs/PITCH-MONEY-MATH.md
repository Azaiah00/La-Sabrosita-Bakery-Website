## Where money is leaving today

### 1 · Marketplace commission

DoorDash and Grubhub marketplace rates run roughly **15% on pickup and 25–30% on delivery**. *(Confirm the exact rate from their merchant statement — plans vary.)*

Direct ordering on their own domain costs **Stripe's 2.9% + $0.30**.

| Monthly marketplace volume | At 25% blended | Same volume direct | Kept per year |
|---|---|---|---|
| $3,000 | $750/mo | ~$117/mo | **~$7,600** |
| $6,000 | $1,500/mo | ~$219/mo | **~$15,400** |
| $10,000 | $2,500/mo | ~$350/mo | **~$25,800** |

*Direct cost assumes an average ticket of $25, so 2.9% + $0.30 per order.*

Keep DoorDash for genuine delivery reach. Just stop paying a quarter of the ticket on customers who were already going to drive over.

**The dashboard tracks this number every month, automatically, and shows it on the front page.**

### 2 · Custom cakes taken by phone

Every custom cake today is a phone call during service — transcribed by hand, no deposit, no written spec.

That costs three ways: staff time during the rush; orders lost because nobody answered at 7 PM on a Saturday when a mother was planning a quinceañera; and remakes when the inscription was heard wrong.

A configurator takes the order at 11 PM, captures the spec in the customer's own words, and holds a deposit. Google's own review-theme data shows *birthday cake* (16 mentions) and *cake decoration* (8) among the top topics — **the demand is already there and it is being handled by phone.**

### 3 · The wholesale book has no digital surface

~250 accounts. No line sheet, no reorder portal, no order history, no invoice tracking, no aging report.

Every reorder is a phone call. Every invoice is chased manually. Nobody knows which account has not ordered in six weeks until they notice the van is lighter.

**No competitor in this market has anything like a wholesale portal.** This is the widest moat available and it is sitting unbuilt.

### 4 · Prices roughly half of reality

Their own website lists **Quesadilla Salvadoreña Entera at $6.80**. DoorDash today lists it at **$13.99**. Tres leches: $2.99 on the site, $5.99 on DoorDash. Flan: $2.99 versus $6.99.

Every published price is roughly 50% of current. Anyone who checks the website before driving over arrives expecting to pay half — which is a customer-service problem at the register, every single day.

### 5 · Nobody knows the food cost

There is no recipe costing, no inventory tracking, no waste log. Standard for an independent bakery — and it means nobody can answer "which item am I selling at a loss?"

Nine out of ten independent bakeries have at least one. Finding it usually pays for a year of software.

## What they get

**Phase 1 — the storefront.** A bilingual site with a real, photographed, searchable menu, cake pages, and complete local-SEO markup. Menus in HTML, not a PDF. Mobile-first, because more than 70% of this traffic is someone deciding in under 20 seconds on a phone.

**Phase 2 — ordering.** Custom cake configurator with deposits. Everyday pickup ordering. Order status by text. This is where the commission bleeding stops.

**Phase 3 — the Bakery OS.** Ingredient inventory with real unit conversions. Recipe costing, so changing the price of butter once re-computes the margin on every product. Production planning. Vendor purchase orders. Waste tracking. Daily sales, expenses, and a real P&L with food cost %, labor % and prime cost.

**Phase 4 — wholesale.** Line sheet, account portal, standing orders, route sheets, invoicing, and an aging report. The 250-account book, finally on a screen.

## What this actually is

It is not a website. A website is the part the customer sees.

Underneath it is the thing that tells a family business — one that started in a home kitchen and now supplies 250 stores — **what it costs to make a concha, which products make money, what got thrown away last week, and what is actually left at the end of the month.**

Most bakeries this size never get that. They run on instinct and a cash drawer, and instinct is usually right about the bread and wrong about the margins.
